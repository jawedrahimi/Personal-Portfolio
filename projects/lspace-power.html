01 // MISSION

Mission Context

The team developed a rover concept for the PNS02 region near the lunar south pole. The science concept combined lunar interior investigation with volatile characterization, including water-ice mapping in permanently shadowed regions and early-stage ISRU feasibility.

02 // MY ROLE

Electrical Power Engineering

I supported the rover Power Subsystem PDR, translating mission constraints into electrical requirements and developing the subsystem concept across generation, storage, regulation, distribution, monitoring, fault recovery, procurement, and verification.

03 // ARCHITECTURE

Generation, Storage & Distribution

The baseline architecture used solar generation as the primary energy source and rechargeable storage to support operations during reduced illumination and permanently shadowed-region traverses. The design also required regulated power distribution to rover subsystems and electrical monitoring for fault detection and energy management.

Solar-array generation requirement: approximately 300–500 W under applicable illuminated operating conditions.
04 // REQUIREMENTS

Power Requirements

The PDR formalized requirements for generation, rechargeable storage, regulated distribution, safe-power behavior, health monitoring, electromagnetic compatibility, subsystem mass, and modular fault isolation.

05 // OPERATIONS

Operating Modes & Power Budget

Power planning considered changing rover demand across mobility, science, thermal control, communications, charging, and PSR operations. The mission concept required strategic load scheduling because solar availability, thermal survival, and science operations compete for limited energy.

06 // VERIFICATION

Verification Strategy

Verification progresses from analysis and inspection to component, subsystem, and integrated testing. Planned checks cover photovoltaic generation, battery storage, regulation, distribution, monitoring, fault protection, electrical interfaces, environmental performance, and electromagnetic compatibility.

  • Measure voltage, current, startup behavior, steady-state power, and shutdown behavior on protected branches.
  • Exercise representative high-current and switching loads while monitoring sensitive instrumentation for interference.
  • Demonstrate end-to-end generation, charging, stored-energy operation, regulation, monitoring, switching, and fault isolation.
